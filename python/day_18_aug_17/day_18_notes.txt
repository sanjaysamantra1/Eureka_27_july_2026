Date Python
============
-The datetime module is the standard way to work with dates and times.

-Key classes: date (year/month/day), time (hour/min/sec), datetime (both combined), timedelta (a duration/difference), timezone (UTC offsets).

-Dates are immutable — every operation returns a new object rather than modifying in place.
datetime.now() gives local time; datetime.utcnow() (deprecated in 3.12+) — use datetime.now(timezone.utc) instead for timezone-aware UTC.

-Naive vs aware: a naive datetime has no timezone info; an aware one does. Mixing the two in comparisons raises errors — always be consistent.

-Formatting uses strftime (datetime → string) and parsing uses strptime (string → datetime). Mnemonic: format, parse.

-For real-world timezone handling, use the built-in zoneinfo module (3.9+) instead of pytz.

-timedelta supports arithmetic directly — add/subtract it from dates, or subtract two dates to get a timedelta.
